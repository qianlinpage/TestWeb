Research projects

Kernel methods and spectral algorithms

We study how regularization, dimension and the spectrum of the data shape statistical learning. This project connects kernel ridge regression, interpolation and spectral algorithms through their learning curves, convergence rates and saturation effects.

Research questions

  • How do source conditions and model misspecification affect optimal learning rates?
  • How do interpolation, saturation and benign overfitting interact in large dimensions?
  • When can sequence models describe the risk of kernel regression?

Deep learning theory and adaptive features

We investigate generalization in neural networks, the scope of neural tangent kernel descriptions, and the role of learned features. Overparameterized sequence and RKHS models provide settings for studying how feature adaptation changes statistical performance.

Adaptive Feature project website

Research questions

  • What can neural tangent kernel theory explain, and which aspects of learning require a description of changing features?
  • How do overparameterization and feature learning improve adaptivity?
  • How do initialization and the alignment of learned kernels affect generalization?

High-dimensional statistics and sufficient dimension reduction

We develop statistical theory for recovering low-dimensional structure from high-dimensional predictors. Sliced inverse regression and single-index models are central examples, with extensions from sparse finite-dimensional settings to functional data.

Research questions

  • When is sliced inverse regression consistent and statistically optimal in high dimensions?
  • How can sparsity and structural dimension be incorporated into dimension reduction?
  • How do these ideas extend to functional predictors and single-index model inference?
